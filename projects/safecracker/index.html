summary

Pressing a button never feels as good as the thing it stands for. Safecracker is an argument for custom sensors — a heist game you play by ear, holding a real stethoscope against a real safe and listening for the tumblers to fall.

Three of us built it at Georgia Tech’s Interactive Product Design Lab over two years. It took first place in the CHI 2020 Student Game Competition, out of more than 50 entries in the Innovative Interfaces category.

my role

I designed and prototyped the physical gameplay — where the stethoscope listens, what the dial and handle do, and how the safe answers when you land on a number.

I also wrote the main game loop in Processing and modeled the final iteration in CAD, routing the exterior on a CNC and printing mounts for every board and motor inside it.

process

technical spikes

We drew and spiked the safe's mechanisms — touch points, door lock, handle lock — to de-risk them before committing to fabrication.

prototype build

A boxy first build integrated every system. Not pretty, but perfect for testing whether the interactions actually held up.

how to play

find the sweet spots

Hold the stethoscope to the marked points on the safe and listen through the embedded Bluetooth headphones.

listen closely

Rotate the dial through the clicks. A solid THUNK marks the next number in the combination.

crack the code

Solve the three-digit combination and the safe opens onto a hidden screen full of treasure.
